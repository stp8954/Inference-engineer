# Decode bandwidth ceilings (batch = 1)

Idealized batch-1 weight-streaming ceiling: tok/s ≈ sustained memory bandwidth (GB/s) ÷ weight
bytes (GB). This isolates the cost of reading weights once per decode step; it is not a performance
prediction when KV traffic, runtime overhead, communication, or unused bandwidth are material.

- 8B model @ BF16 (~16 GB of weights) on H100 SXM (~3.35 TB/s peak bandwidth) → ~210 tok/s idealized weight-bandwidth ceiling. [sourced] — vendor bandwidth spec plus the formula above; needs a pinned checkpoint and [verified] KVScope run before being presented as measured performance. Recorded 2026-08-22; wording clarified 2026-08-24.
- 8B @ BF16 on current MacBook Pro configurations: base M5 at 153 GB/s → ~9.6 tok/s; highest-bandwidth M5 Max at 614 GB/s → ~38.4 tok/s idealized weight-bandwidth ceilings, provided the selected unified-memory capacity holds the checkpoint plus runtime/KV state. [sourced] — Apple MacBook Pro technical specifications (see ../../sources/2026-08-24-apple-macbook-pro-specs.md); these are not measured inference results. Recorded 2026-08-24.
- Per-token dense linear-layer work ≈ 2 FLOPs/parameter → ~16 GFLOP for 8B. Against 989 TFLOP/s peak, that is an idealized ~16 μs compute-time floor; at the separate ~210 tok/s weight-bandwidth ceiling, it would demand ~3.4 TFLOP/s, or ~0.34% of peak. [sourced] — standard matrix-multiply estimate plus the H100 vendor peak specification; derivation in the Week 1 draft. Recorded 2026-08-22; dimensional comparison corrected 2026-08-24.
- H100 ops:byte ratio ≈ 295 (989 TFLOPS dense FP16 ÷ 3.35 TB/s): any op with arithmetic intensity below ~295 is memory-bound on H100; standard attention at decode (d=128, N=4096, FP16) has AI ≈ 62. [sourced] — Kiely, Inference Engineering (2026) §2.4 (see ../../sources/2026-08-22-kiely-inference-engineering.md). Recorded 2026-08-22.
- 7B @ FP16 (14 GB) on H100: 14/3350 ≈ 4.18 ms/token ITL floor ≈ 239 tok/s ceiling — independent source, same formula as the 8B entry. [sourced] — Vizuara Ch 2. Recorded 2026-08-22.
- Batch-1 ITL floors on H100 for a 7B model by precision: FP16 (14 GB) 4.18 ms; FP8 2.09 ms; INT4 1.05 ms. Compute utilization at batch 1 ≈ 0.003% (28 GFLOPs needed vs 989 TFLOPS available). [sourced] — Vizuara §6.6. Recorded 2026-08-22.
- Continuous batching at 32 users amortizes the same weight load to ~0.13 ms/token effective. [sourced] — Vizuara §6.6. Recorded 2026-08-22.
## The formula measured against reality

- **The strongest evidence we have that the ceiling predicts rather than merely bounds.** Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2 at FP16 (~15 GB of weights, FP16 KV cache, 4K context) on an RTX 4090 (1,008 GB/s): stated weight-bandwidth ceiling **67.1 tok/s**; a hand-written C++/CUDA implementation reached **63.8 tok/s** at short context — **~95% of the ceiling** — and the `calm` engine reached **66.0 tok/s (~98%)**. llama.cpp on the same box: 61.0 tok/s (~91%). [sourced] — Chan, *YALM* (2026) (see ../../sources/2026-08-24-chan-yalm.md). Recorded 2026-08-25. **This is the entry that licenses presenting the Week 1 ceiling as a prediction rather than a loose upper bound. It does not substitute for our own 8B/H100 run, which still needs to move to [verified] on its own evidence.**
- Same machine, CPU side: AMD EPYC 7702P at 204.8 GB/s → ~13.7 tok/s predicted ceiling for the same 15 GB checkpoint; the optimized CPU implementation reached 8.2–8.4 tok/s (~61% of ceiling), llama.cpp 8.7 (~64%). CPU implementations land materially further below their ceiling than GPU ones. [sourced] — Chan, *YALM* (2026) for the bandwidth and measured rates; the ceiling is our arithmetic from the stated 204.8 GB/s. Recorded 2026-08-25.
- Long-context erosion in the same run: 63.8 tok/s short → **58.8 tok/s** long (4K context), i.e. the KV-traffic term becoming visible exactly as the Week 1 derivation predicts it should. `calm` erodes far less (66.0 → 65.7). [sourced] — Chan, *YALM* (2026). Recorded 2026-08-25.

## The offload regime (weights exceed VRAM)

- **Both terms of the formula are replaced.** The bandwidth becomes the slowest tier a token must cross — host memory over PCIe rather than HBM — and for an MoE the bytes are only the *active, non-resident* experts rather than the whole checkpoint. Expert-cache hit rate, not FLOPs, is the design variable. [sourced] — derivation applied to the regime measured in FreeToken (see ../../sources/2026-08-25-yang-freetoken.md). Recorded 2026-08-25.
- **The effective ceiling in that regime is host-memory bandwidth, not PCIe.** FreeToken's `q⋆` derivation: since expert DMA and CPU expert execution draw on the same host-memory subsystem, a saturated PCIe link leaves residual bandwidth `B_R = max(B_H − B_P, 0)` for concurrent CPU execution. Balancing `T_fill(q) = qS/B_P` against `T_cpu(m−q) = (m−q)S/(B_H − B_P)` gives **`q⋆ ≈ m · B_P / B_H`** — the fraction of the `m` missed experts that should be filled over PCIe rather than executed in place on the CPU. As `B_H → B_P` the policy degenerates to pure cache fill. [sourced] — FreeToken §3.2, eqs. 1–4. Recorded 2026-08-25.
- Consequence worth stating in Week 1: on these machines `B_H` exceeds `B_P` by roughly 1.5–4×, so a system that treats every miss as a transfer is capped at the PCIe number while host compute sits idle. That is the ceiling FreeToken's competitors are pinned to. [sourced] — FreeToken §3.2 and Table 1. Recorded 2026-08-25.
- Long-context reality check, Llama-3-70B @32K on H100 (2-way TP): 43 GB KV re-read (12.8 ms) + weights (~21 ms) ≈ 34 ms single-user ITL — KV traffic dominates past N≈4K. [sourced] — Vizuara §7.7. Recorded 2026-08-22.
